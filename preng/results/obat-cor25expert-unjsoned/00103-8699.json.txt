Since $ \gcd(2, 100) = 2 $, Euler's theorem does not directly apply. However, we can use **modular exponentiation** to compute $ 2^{99} \mod 100 $.

We can break this into:

$$
2^{99} = 2^{40} \cdot 2^{40} \cdot 2^9
$$

We compute each part modulo 100:

- $ 2^{10} = 1024 \Rightarrow 2^{10} \mod 100 = 24 $
- $ 2^{20} = (2^{10})^2 = 24^2 = 576 \Rightarrow 576 \mod 100 = 76 $
- $ 2^{40} = (2^{20})^2 = 76^2 = 5776 \Rightarrow 5776 \mod 100 = 76 $
- $ 2^9 = 512 \Rightarrow 512 \mod 100 = 12 $

$$
2^{99} = 2^{40} \cdot 2^{40} \cdot 2^9 \mod 100 = 76 \cdot 76 \cdot 12 \mod 100
$$

- $ 76 \cdot 76 = 5776 \Rightarrow 5776 \mod 100 = 76 $
