To determine the number of ways to climb a staircase of **10 steps**, where you can take **either 1 or 2 steps at a time**, and **you cannot descend**, we are dealing with a classic **combinatorial problem** often referred to as the **Fibonacci staircase problem**.

### 🧠 Understanding the Problem

At each step, you have two choices:
- Take **1 step** (move from step $ n $ to $ n+1 $)
- Take **2 steps** (move from step $ n $ to $ n+2 $)

You **cannot go backward**, so the number of ways to reach step $ n $ is the sum of:
- The number of ways to reach step $ n-1 $ (then take 1 step)
- The number of ways to reach step $ n-2 $ (then take 2 steps)

This gives us the recurrence relation:

$$
a(n) = a(n-1) + a(n-2)
$$

With base cases:
- $ a(0) = 1 $ (1 way to stay at the ground level)
- $ a(1) = 1 $ (1 way to reach step 1)

This is the **Fibonacci sequence**, shifted by one index.
