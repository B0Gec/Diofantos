To solve the problem of determining which group a given position `n` belongs to, we need to find the smallest integer `m` such that the sum of the first `m` natural numbers is at least `n`. This is because the k-th group contains `k` elements, and the total number of elements up to the m-th group is the sum of the first `m` natural numbers, i.e., `m * (m + 1) // 2`.

---

### **Key Insight**

We are looking for the smallest integer `m` such that:

$$
\frac{m(m + 1)}{2} \geq n
$$

This is a classic problem that can be efficiently solved using **binary search** to avoid potential floating-point precision issues that may arise with large values of `n`.

---

### **Approach**

1. **Binary Search Setup**:
   - Set `low = 1` and `high = 2 * 10^9` as an upper bound (since for `n = 10^18`, the corresponding `m` is about `1.4 * 10^9`).
   - While `low < high`, compute the midpoint `mid`.
   - Calculate the sum of the first `mid` natural numbers: `mid * (mid + 1) // 2`.
   - If this sum is greater than or equal to `n`, move the upper bound to `mid`.
   - Otherwise, move the lower bound to `mid + 1`.

2. **Termination**:
   - When the loop ends, `low` will be the smallest integer `m` such that the sum of the first `m` natural numbers is at least `n`.

---

### **Python Implementation**

```python
n = int(input())

low = 1
high = 2 * 10**9  # A sufficiently large upper bound

while low < high:
    mid = (low + high) // 2
    total = mid * (mid + 1) // 2
    if total >= n:
        high = mid
    else:
        low = mid + 1

print(low)
```

---

### **Example**

For `n = 5`:
- The sum of the first 2 natural numbers is 3, which is less than 5.
- The sum of the first 3 natural numbers is 6, which is greater than or equal to 5.
- So, the correct group is `3`.

---

### **Time Complexity**

- **Binary Search**: `O(log N)`, where `N` is the upper bound (`2 * 10^9`), which is very efficient even for large values of `n`.

---

### **Conclusion**

This binary search approach is both **accurate** and **efficient**, avoiding any issues with floating-point precision and ensuring correctness for all valid inputs, including very large values of `n`.
