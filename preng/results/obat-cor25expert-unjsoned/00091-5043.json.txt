To define the sequence of **central binomial coefficients** recursively, we observe that the $ n $-th term of the sequence is given by:

$$
a_n = \binom{n}{\left\lfloor \frac{n}{2} \right\rfloor}
$$

This sequence begins as:

$$
a_0 = 1,\quad a_1 = 1,\quad a_2 = 2,\quad a_3 = 3,\quad a_4 = 6,\quad a_5 = 10,\quad a_6 = 20,\quad a_7 = 35,\quad a_8 = 70,\quad a_9 = 126,\quad \dots
$$

---

### **Recursive Definition**

We can define the sequence recursively using the following piecewise recurrence:

- **Base cases**:
  $$
  a_0 = 1,\quad a_1 = 1
  $$

- **For $ n \geq 2 $**:
  $$
  a_n = 
  \begin{cases}
  2 \cdot a_{n-1}, & \text{if } n \text{ is even} \\
  \dfrac{2n}{n+1} \cdot a_{n-1}, & \text{if } n \text{ is odd}
  \end{cases}
  $$

---

### **Explanation**

- **Even $ n $**: When $ n $ is even, the central binomial coefficient $ \binom{n}{n/2} $ is exactly double the previous term $ \binom{n-1}{(n-1)/2} $. This is because the binomial coefficient grows by a factor of 2 when moving from an odd to an even index in this sequence.

- **Odd $ n $**: When $ n $ is odd, the central binomial coefficient $ \binom{n}{(n-1)/2} $ is related to the previous term $ \binom{n-1}{(n-1)/2} $ by a rational factor $ \dfrac{2n}{n+1} $. This factor arises from the recursive identity of binomial coefficients.

---

### **Example Calculation**

Let’s compute $ a_5 $ using the recurrence:

- $ a_4 = 6 $ (even, so $ a_4 = 2 \cdot a_3 = 2 \cdot 3 = 6 $)
- $ a_5 = \dfrac{2 \cdot 5}{5 + 1} \cdot a_4 = \dfrac{10}{6} \cdot 6 = 10 $

This matches the known value $ \binom{5}{2} = 10 $.
